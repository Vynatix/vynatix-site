# Security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

Email **front.disk@vynatix.com** with the details. We reply to inbound within
one working day and will tell you what we can fix and when.

The same contact is published in machine-readable form at
[`/.well-known/security.txt`](.well-known/security.txt) per RFC 9116.

Please include what you found, how to reproduce it, and what you think the
impact is. If a finding needs a proof of concept, keep it to the minimum needed
to demonstrate the issue — do not access data that is not yours, degrade the
service, or run automated scanning that generates load.

We will not pursue action against anyone who reports a genuine issue in good
faith and gives us reasonable time to fix it before disclosing.

## Scope

This repository is the **public marketing website** at `vynatix.com`. It is a
static site: flat HTML, CSS and one vanilla JavaScript file of our own, with no
server-side code, no database, no authentication, no forms and no user accounts.
There is nothing here that processes a submission. The one third-party script,
Google's `gtag.js`, is loaded by that file only after a visitor accepts
analytics, and only once a Measurement ID has been set (see below).

In scope: anything served from `vynatix.com`, such as content injection, a broken
Content-Security-Policy, a supply-chain problem in a shipped asset, exposure
of something that should not be published, or anything that requests Google or
sets an analytics cookie before the visitor has accepted. That last one is a
bug in the consent module and we treat it as a vulnerability.

Out of scope: findings that require a compromised visitor device, social
engineering of our staff, denial of service through volume, missing headers that
GitHub Pages cannot send (documented under "Known and accepted" below), and
reports generated by a scanner with no demonstrated impact.

## What this site does deliberately

- **One third-party origin family, and only on request.** Fonts and images are
  served first-party. The Content-Security-Policy names Google's analytics hosts
  (`www.googletagmanager.com`, `*.google-analytics.com`,
  `*.analytics.google.com`) for Google Analytics, and nothing else. Nothing is
  requested from them, and no cookie is set, until the visitor has accepted on the
  consent bar (on the press, or on a later visit while the stored Accept, valid for
  up to 365 days, has not lapsed). A visitor who declines, or who has not chosen,
  discloses nothing to anyone but our host.
- **Analytics is opt-in and inert until switched on.** The consent module in
  `app.js` ships with a placeholder Measurement ID and does nothing while it is
  the placeholder: no bar, no storage access, no request. Once a real ID is set,
  the choice is kept only in the visitor's own browser (local storage key
  `vynatix-consent`, for up to 365 days), is never sent to us, and can be
  withdrawn from the footer of every page. Declining or withdrawing stops the
  measurement and deletes the Google Analytics cookies.
- **A strict CSP**, delivered per page as a `<meta>` tag: `script-src 'self'`
  plus the one Google script path `/gtag/js`, and `style-src 'self'` with no
  `'unsafe-inline'`, `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`.
  The Google exception is a recorded security downgrade from the earlier
  `'self'`-only policy; see "Known and accepted" and Round 6 of
  `SECURITY-ASSESSMENT.md`.
- **No other analytics, cookies or tracking.** No advertising tags and no social
  widgets. Google's advertising features are off: Signals and ad personalisation
  are switched off in the tag configuration, and the pre-flip checklist
  (`tools/PRE-FLIP-CHECKLIST.md`) requires them off in the Google property too
  before analytics is switched on.
- **Developer documentation is not published.** `_config.yml` keeps this file,
  `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, the security assessment and `tools/` out of the
  deployed site.

## Known and accepted

Some controls are HTTP response headers, and GitHub Pages cannot send them:
HSTS, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `X-Frame-Options` / `frame-ancestors`
(clickjacking), `Permissions-Policy`, and CSP violation reporting. These are
being addressed by putting a header-capable edge in front of the origin; until
that is live, please treat them as known rather than new findings.

The Content-Security-Policy's Google hosts are also known. The wildcard
`connect-src` hosts would let a script that ran on a page send data to any Google
Analytics property, and a `<meta>` policy cannot depend on consent, so the same
policy reaches every visitor. The page has no known way to run an attacker's
script, which is why we accepted it; the trade-off is written down in
`SECURITY-ASSESSMENT.md` (Round 6). A way to run script on the site, or any
request to Google before the visitor has accepted, is a finding.
